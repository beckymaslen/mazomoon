# A Court of Frost and Starlight Review: The Book I Couldn't Finish for Two Years

*Spoiler-free review first, followed by a clearly marked discussion of ACOFAS.*  
*Contains spoilers for Books 1 to 3, as this is set after them. No spoilers from A Court of Silver Flames.*

In July 2024, I read *A Court of Mist and Fury* and *A Court of Wings and Ruin* back to back. After needing three attempts to get into the first book, I'd become the sort of person who reads fantasy novels while the kettle boils, while the dinner cooks and, on one occasion, while technically supposed to be doing something else entirely.

So you'd think a short Winter Solstice novella set in Velaris would have taken me an afternoon.

It took me two years.

## What is A Court of Frost and Starlight about?

*A Court of Frost and Starlight*, usually shortened to ACOFAS, sits between *A Court of Wings and Ruin* and *A Court of Silver Flames*. It's a novella, much shorter than the main books, and it's set a few months after the war with Hybern.

Prythian is rebuilding. Velaris is repairing the damage from the attack, and the Night Court is preparing for its first Winter Solstice since everything changed. Feyre is learning what it means to be High Lady, Rhysand is learning what peace looks like, and everyone in the Inner Circle is dealing with the aftermath in their own way. Some of them are dealing with it considerably better than others.

There's no great villain to defeat and no war to win. It's a quieter, smaller story about what happens after the battle is over, told in chapters from several members of the Inner Circle rather than Feyre alone.

## A very different kind of ACOTAR book

After the scale of ACOWAR, with its armies, ancient creatures and emotional devastation, ACOFAS is a complete change of pace. It's shopping for Solstice presents, snow over Velaris, family dinners and long conversations.
