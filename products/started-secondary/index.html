Sound familiar?

New building, new people, new everything

Starting secondary school is a big deal. There are new routines, a timetable, getting lost between lessons, and working out where they fit, all at once.

A lot of that goes unsaid. Children often keep the worries in their head, where they feel bigger than they are.

A gentler way

Somewhere to put it all down

This journal gives them a private place for the questions, the worries, the small wins and the moments worth remembering. They can fill it in before they start, during the first weeks, or whenever they need it.

There’s no right way to use it. Skip pages, come back later, write loads or a little. It’s theirs.

I made this journal for the move that feels huge at eleven. I wanted it to say clearly, on the very first page, that nobody has to have it all figured out on day one.

It’s meant to be private, so it’s worth telling them that it’s theirs and nobody will read it unless they want them to.

Questions

Things people ask me

When should I give it to them?

Any time from the end of Year 6 to the first term of Year 7. Some pages are for before they start, and lots are for the first weeks, but they can start whenever they like.

Is it only for nervous children?

No. Plenty of children are excited, and the journal keeps the good moments too. It just also has room for the wobbles.

Will they need help filling it in?

Most eleven-year-olds can use it on their own. It’s designed to be private, so it’s best to let them choose what to share.

Is it the same as a school planner?

No. There are no homework or timetable pages. It’s a journal about how they’re feeling and what they’re noticing.

Does it work for a new school in another year?

It’s written for starting secondary, but a child moving schools at any age would find most of the pages useful.
