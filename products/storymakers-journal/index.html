Sound familiar?

“I don’t know what to write”

Most children love making things up, until someone hands them a blank page and says “write a story”. Then the ideas vanish.

Starting is the hardest part. A whole story feels like too much, so they don’t start at all.

A gentler way

Build the story in small pieces

Instead of asking for a whole story, this journal starts small: invent a character, draw a map, try a comic challenge. Bit by bit, they collect the pieces of a story without noticing.

Then they turn their favourite idea into a story of their very own. Some pages are marked TOGETHER, for doing side by side with a grown-up.

What it is

A toolkit for young storytellers

Prompts that do the hard part, starting, so the fun part can take over.

Questions

Things people ask me

What age is it for?

It suits children from about seven to eleven. Younger children can draw their answers and do the TOGETHER pages with a grown-up.

My child doesn’t like writing. Will they enjoy it?

There’s plenty of drawing: characters, maps and comics. Many children end up writing more because they’ve got something they want to say.

How is it different from The Storymaker’s Comic Book?

This journal is about inventing characters, worlds and story ideas, with a little comic making. The Comic Book is A4 and is all comics: twelve to make from start to finish.

Do they need to do it in order?

No. Every page works on its own, so they can jump to whatever looks fun.

Is it useful for school?

It isn’t a workbook, but the habits it builds, like planning a character or a setting, are the same ones used in creative writing at school.
