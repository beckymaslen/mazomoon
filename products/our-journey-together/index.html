Sound familiar?

“How was school?” “Fine.”

You know there’s more going on in their head. But the questions you ask at teatime get one-word answers, and the big conversations seem to happen at bedtime when everyone’s tired.

It isn’t that they don’t want to share. Talking face to face can just feel like a lot.

A gentler way

Pass a book back and forth instead

Dark pages are for you, light pages are for your child, and pages marked TOGETHER are for both of you. Fill in your page, then leave the journal somewhere for them to find: under a pillow, in a drawer, on the stairs.

There’s no pressure and no right way to do it. Over time it becomes a slow conversation on paper that you’ll both want to keep.

What it is

A quiet way to get to know each other better

Light, playful prompts on every page, so it never feels like an interview.

  1. 01

    Pages for each of you

    Dark pages for the grown-up, light pages for the child, so everyone has their own say.

  2. 02

    Pages to do together

    A family tree to draw, and lists to tick and compare, like “Yes, please! or No, thanks!”

  3. 03

    Easy, fun questions

    Top places you’ve visited, who you’d be if you were a cartoon, what you love right now.

I made this journal for the conversations that are hard to start. Some children say far more with a pen than they ever would across the dinner table.

I kept the questions light on purpose. The deeper stuff tends to come out on its own once you’ve both got into the habit.

Questions

Things people ask me

What age is it for?

It works best from around seven, once your child can write a few sentences on their own. Younger children can draw their answers or tell you what to write.

Does it have to be a parent?

Not at all. It works for any grown-up and child: grandparents, step-parents, aunties, uncles, godparents or carers.

Do we have to do it in order?

No. Pick any page you like. The only rule is that you pass it back when you’re done.

What if my child doesn’t want to write much?

That’s fine. Many pages can be answered with a tick, a word or a drawing. The point is the connection, not the handwriting.

Is it a good gift?

Yes, it makes a thoughtful gift for a birthday or Christmas, or for a grandparent who lives far away to post back and forth.
