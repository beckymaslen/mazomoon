Sound familiar?

Holidays go by so fast, and the travelling bit can feel so slow

Long journeys make little people restless. Screens fill the gap, but they don’t leave anything behind when the battery runs out.

And once you’re home, the trip blurs. The photos stay on a phone, and the funny thing that happened on day three gets forgotten by October.

A gentler way

Give them something to do on the way, and something to keep afterwards

This journal follows the trip in order: getting ready, on the way, and once you’ve arrived. The travel pages are full of games, and the holiday pages are short and simple, so filling them in feels like fun rather than homework.

By the end they’ve made their own record of the holiday, in their own handwriting.

A note from Rebecca

I made this journal so the journey becomes part of the holiday rather than the bit everyone puts up with. Every page has a job: keep hands busy on the way, or keep a memory once you’re there.

I designed every page myself, and I kept the writing spaces small enough that nobody feels they have to fill a whole page.

Questions

Things people ask me

What age is it for?

It’s written for primary school children who can read and write a little. Younger children can do it with a grown-up helping with the writing, and the puzzles work for everyone.

Is it for one holiday or lots of trips?

It’s designed to cover one holiday from start to finish, with a page for each day. Many families buy one per trip so each holiday gets its own book.

Does it work for a holiday at home in the UK?

Yes. Nothing in it assumes you’re going abroad. The flag matching is just a puzzle, and the daily pages work for a caravan in Whitby as well as a flight to Spain.

Can they use felt tips?

Pencils, crayons and gel pens are best. Heavy felt tips can show through to the other side of the page.

Does it come gift wrapped?

Amazon sometimes offers gift options at checkout. The book arrives plain, so it’s ready to wrap yourself.
